## What was measured, and what this is not

- **Signals.** The brief's six, per image, pooled over windows of 40 images: confidence drift (KS statistic and mean shift of candidate confidences against 300 seeded train images), augmentation disagreement (flip, 0.75x scale, 1.3x brightness), temporal inconsistency (**still-image proxy**: three shaken, re-noised copies; the datasets have no video), person-detector disagreement (COCO YOLO11n, conf 0.40, IoU 0.30), orphan PPE (ppe.associate rule) and C2PSA feature Mahalanobis distance.
- **Target.** Recall drop at the operating confidence 0.28, paired against the same images clean, positive = recall lost. Two targets: `all` (micro recall over 11 classes) and `violation` (pooled recall of no_helmet, no_goggle, no_gloves, no_boots).
- **Fit and hold-out.** Ridge (alpha 1, fixed before scoring) fitted on val windows only; leave-one-condition-out: each condition is scored on test (golden v1) windows by a model that never saw that condition. Test is used only for scoring. **This is not the brief's held-out site**; that number is missing (`results/missing.md`).
- **Baselines.** AC (difference of average confidence) and ATC (average thresholded confidence), both with clean val as source, neither fitted on shift.
- **Check.** Test clean micro recall from the cached records is 0.715428, identical to Phase 2's clean reference (run p2_shift_yolo11s_20261007T181833Z).
- **Sample size.** 141 test images per cell; 18 shifted cells; 20 windows of 40 images per cell (windows share images, so they are not independent; intervals below resample whole cells).

## Result 1: estimation error, target `all`

Mean absolute error of the estimated recall drop over the 18 shifted test cells (360 windows). CI = 95%, 1,000 resamples of the 18 cells.

| method | window MAE [95% CI] | bias | full-cell MAE | Spearman over cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| six signals, condition held out | 0.0896 [0.0678, 0.1152] | +0.0485 | 0.0532 | 0.889 |
| AC baseline | 0.1466 [0.0931, 0.2113] | -0.1459 | 0.1395 | 0.837 |
| ATC baseline | 0.1643 [0.1064, 0.2353] | -0.1624 | 0.1564 | 0.383 |
| confidence drift only (same protocol) | 0.0833 [0.0734, 0.0947] | +0.0322 | 0.0388 | 0.953 |
| augmentation disagreement only | 0.0787 [0.0495, 0.1162] | +0.0026 | 0.0708 | 0.794 |
| temporal proxy only | 0.0832 [0.0595, 0.1130] | -0.0127 | 0.0716 | 0.684 |
| feature distance only | 0.0988 [0.0625, 0.1456] | +0.0291 | 0.0917 | 0.682 |
| person-detector disagreement only | 0.1066 [0.0873, 0.1279] | +0.0272 | 0.0949 | 0.424 |
| orphan PPE only | 0.1244 [0.0934, 0.1611] | +0.0292 | 0.1140 | 0.154 |
| six signals, condition seen in fit (image hold-out only) | 0.0767 [0.0636, 0.0902] | +0.0515 | 0.0338 | 0.936 |

What it shows:
- AC and ATC, unfitted, **underestimate** every large drop (low light s3: true 0.488, AC 0.101, ATC 0.058). Confidence falls far less than recall does.
- A fitted regressor on label-free signals tracks the drop much better (full-cell MAE 0.053 vs 0.140 and 0.156).
- **Negative result: the six-signal model is not better than confidence drift alone** under this hold-out (0.0896 vs 0.0833 window MAE, overlapping intervals; full-cell 0.053 vs 0.039). The extra signals do not add measurable information across unseen corruption types, and person-detector disagreement and orphan PPE are weak on their own (Spearman 0.42 and 0.15).
- The largest single errors are motion blur s3 (true 0.424, estimated 0.215: underestimated) and downscale s1 (true 0.020, estimated 0.168: false drop).

Per-cell values with 95% intervals over images: `--table` output, and `est_vs_true_all.png`.

## Result 2: estimation error, target `violation` (negative)

| method | window MAE [95% CI] | bias | full-cell MAE | Spearman over cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| six signals, condition held out | 0.1423 [0.1032, 0.1987] | +0.1146 | 0.1076 | 0.491 |
| AC baseline | 0.1136 [0.0880, 0.1424] | -0.0989 | 0.0792 | -0.038 |
| ATC baseline | 0.1216 [0.0947, 0.1476] | -0.0694 | 0.0755 | -0.031 |
| temporal proxy only | 0.1076 [0.0831, 0.1428] | +0.0694 | 0.0893 | 0.619 |

- **No method estimates the violation-class drop usefully.** The baselines have lower MAE only because their estimates sit near zero while the true drops are small (clean violation recall is already 0.18 to 0.30, so it has little room to fall); their rank correlation with the truth is zero.
- The signal model ranks cells better (Spearman 0.49) but overestimates: low light s3 true 0.071, estimated 0.664. Low light collapses overall recall but barely moves the already-low no_* recall, and a model fitted on overall signals cannot tell those apart.
- Sample size is small: 154 violation instances in the test split, about 44 per 40-image window.

## Result 3: lead time (synthetic streams)

20 streams per condition and severity: 8 clean windows, then 10 windows in which 10%..100% of the 40 images are under the condition. Real drop = first window with true drop >= 0.05; alarm = first window with estimate >= 0.05 (threshold fixed before the run). Delay in windows, positive = late. Full table: `--table`; curves: `streams_all.png`.

Target `all`, severity 3:

| stream | streams with a real drop | six signals: mean delay, missed, false-alarm streams | AC | ATC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low_light_s3 | 20 | +0.25, 0, 18 | +5.25, 0, 0 | +4.75, 0, 2 |
| motion_blur_s3 | 20 | +0.40, 0, 17 | +6.83, 14, 0 | +4.17, 14, 0 |
| jpeg_s3 | 20 | +0.30, 0, 16 | +5.35, 0, 0 | +4.05, 1, 5 |
| rain_s3 | 20 | -0.90, 0, 20 | +4.00, 19, 0 | +1.00, 18, 3 |
| downscale_s3 | 20 | -0.50, 0, 18 | +5.42, 1, 0 | +4.35, 3, 6 |
| haze_s3 | 20 | -5.30, 0, 20 | -1.00, 19, 1 | -0.57, 13, 2 |

- The signal monitor raises the alarm within about one window of the real drop and misses none, while AC and ATC are 4 to 7 windows late (160 to 280 frames at 40 frames per window) or miss the drop.
- **Negative result: at this threshold the signal monitor is not deployable.** It alarms during the clean prefix in 16 to 20 of 20 streams. On clean 40-image windows its estimate scatters (window MAE on the clean cell 0.086 vs 0.036 for AC), so a fixed 0.05 threshold fires on noise. A threshold or window size calibrated on val (never on test) is the next step; it was not done here because it was not fixed before this run.
- For the `violation` target every method except AC false-alarms in nearly every stream, consistent with Result 2.

## What this does and does not prove

- Does: on this dataset and model, a regressor fitted on label-free signals estimates the overall recall drop of an unseen synthetic corruption to within about 0.05 to 0.09 absolute recall, clearly better than the two published confidence baselines, which underestimate large drops.
- Does not: say anything about a real new site (the brief's done-criterion). Synthetic corruptions of the same images share scenes, people and camera; a real site changes all of them.
- Does not: support a claim that the six signals together beat confidence drift alone, or that violation-class recall can be monitored without labels.
- Does not: validate the temporal signal on video; it is a still-image proxy.
